How to write a quote for a job: free template and quote maker

A price on a WhatsApp message wins the job until something goes wrong. A written quote that says what's included, what isn't and how payment works wins more jobs and stops most arguments before they start. Here's what to put in it, words to avoid, and a free tool that writes it and checks it for you.

Updated 27 October 2026 · UK · Checked against legislation.gov.uk and gov.uk · General information, not legal advice

The short answer: a good quote has ten parts: your details, a quote number and valid-until date, the customer and job address, a one-line summary, a numbered scope a stranger could tick off, what's not included, what the price assumes, an itemised price with VAT stated, payment terms (deposit, stages, when the balance is due), and how to accept. Call it a quote only if the price is fixed; if it could change, call it an estimate and give an upper limit. If you agree the job in a homeowner's house and it's over £42, they usually have 14 days to cancel, and you must tell them so.

The ten parts of a good quote

PartWhat to writeWhy it matters
1. Your detailsBusiness name, phone, email, address. VAT number if registered; trade registration (Gas Safe, NICEIC) if relevant.Looks professional and tells them who they're dealing with.
2. Number, date, valid until"Q-0042, 27 October 2026, valid until 26 November 2026." 30 days is common; shorter if material prices are moving.Stops a customer accepting a six-month-old price.
3. Customer and job addressFull name and the address where the work is.Matters when the payer and the property differ (landlords, family).
4. The job in a sentenceWhat they'll end up with: "Two new bedroom radiators with thermostatic valves."The customer reads this first.
5. What's includedNumbered lines with quantities, sizes, specs and where. Include prep, protection, waste removal and clean-up if you do them.The line that settles most "I thought you were doing..." arguments.
6. Not includedFive sharp exclusions for this job beat twenty generic ones.Customers assume. This is where you correct them, in advance.
7. AssumptionsWhat the price relies on: access, existing condition, parking, power and water on site.If an assumption turns out wrong, you've already said the price may change.
8. PriceLabour, materials and other costs (skip, hire, disposal) as lines, then the total. Say whether VAT is added, included or not charged.Itemising helps them compare like with like, and shows where the money goes.
9. Payment termsDeposit (amount and what it's for), stage payments tied to things they can see, and when the balance is due. How to pay.Unpaid invoices start with unclear terms (see our late payment guide).
10. Changes, guarantee, how to accept"Changes agreed in writing with a price before we do them." What you guarantee and for how long. "Reply 'I accept' or sign below."A written "yes" is your contract. A text counts.

Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, anything you say or write to a homeowner about the work that they rely on when deciding can become part of the contract (section 50). That cuts both ways: a vague quote binds you to whatever they reasonably understood, and a clear one protects you. If no price is agreed at all, the customer only has to pay a "reasonable price" (section 51), which is a recipe for an argument.

Quote or estimate: which should you call it?

QuoteEstimate
PriceFixed for the work describedA best guess that can change
Use it whenYou can see everything: a boiler swap like for like, painting a room, fitting a supplied kitchenYou can't see what's behind walls, under floors or in the roof: rewiring an old house, a leak you haven't traced, stripping a roof
How to word it"QUOTE. Total £2,450 including VAT.""ESTIMATE. Estimated £1,800–£2,300. We'll call you before going over £2,300."
If the job growsYou absorb it, unless it's outside the scope, an exclusion, or an assumption that proved wrongYou explain, agree the new figure in writing, then carry on

Customers (and courts) tend to treat a "quote" as a fixed price, so don't call something a quote if it isn't. Words like "approx" or "around" in a quote muddle it: the quote maker flags them. A common middle way: quote the part you can see, and add a priced extra or an estimate for the part you can't ("if the joists need replacing, £85 per joist, we'll show you first").

The test: if you were off sick and another tradesperson turned up with only this quote, would they do the same job you priced? If not, add the detail.

Exclusions you might forget, by trade

Pick only the ones that fit this job. The quote maker suggests these when you choose your trade.

  • Every trade: redecoration after the work, moving furniture, hidden problems found once opened up (say you'll stop, show them and price it first), out-of-hours work, permit and building control fees.
  • Plumbing and heating: system flush or powerflush, radiator valves or pipework not listed, upgrading the gas supply, boxing in pipes.
  • Electrical: remedial work found during testing, plastering and painting after chasing, fittings or appliances not listed.
  • Building: structural engineer, architect or party wall surveyor fees, unexpected ground conditions, hazardous waste.
  • Roofing: rotten timbers found once stripped, scaffolding beyond the stated period, gutters and fascias unless listed.
  • Decorating: paint supply, filling beyond minor cracks, wallpaper stripping, ceilings and woodwork unless listed.
  • Landscaping: waste soil beyond the amount stated, rubble or buried services, aftercare and watering after handover.

Deposits, stage payments and when the balance is due

  • Deposit: say what it's for. Covering materials you're ordering for this job is easy to justify; a large deposit with no reason puts customers off, and homeowners are wary of paying most of a job upfront. The quote maker warns above 50% for homeowners.
  • Stage payments for bigger jobs, tied to something the customer can see: "£2,000 when the first fix is complete", not "£2,000 in week 2". You're never more than one stage out of pocket.
  • Balance: give a number of days and how to pay. Homeowners: on completion or within 7 days is common. Businesses: 30 days is usual, and if they pay late you can add statutory interest and compensation (calculator).
  • When the job's done: invoice the same day, quoting the quote number. Our invoice template and maker covers what it must include, VAT, the reverse charge and CIS.
  • Late fees for homeowners only work if they're in the quote or terms before work starts, and reasonable. You can't add one afterwards.

Homeowners' 14 days to cancel (the bit most trades miss)

The Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 apply when you do work for a homeowner (a "consumer") and the contract is agreed away from your business premises, which usually means at their kitchen table after a site visit. They also generally apply to contracts agreed entirely by phone, email or online, if that's how you normally take work.

RuleWhat it means for your quote
14 days to cancelFor a service contract, the customer can cancel within 14 days of agreeing, without a reason (reg 30).
Tell them, in writingGive them the cancellation information and a cancellation form. If you don't, the 14 days can stretch by up to 12 months (reg 31), and they could cancel after the work is done.
Starting within 14 daysOnly if they ask you to, expressly, and for an off-premises contract in a "durable medium" (an email or text is fine). If they then cancel, they pay a proportionate amount for the work done so far (reg 36).
£42 or lessOff-premises contracts where the customer pays £42 or less are outside these rules (reg 7(4)).
Urgent repairsNo right to cancel where the customer asked you to visit to do urgent repairs or maintenance (reg 28(1)(e)). Take care if you add non-urgent extras on the same visit.
Business customersThese rules don't apply.

Business Companion (the trading standards guidance site) has plain-English guidance and the model cancellation form. When the customer is a homeowner and you pick "at their home", the quote maker adds a "Your right to cancel" section to the quote; attach the form with it.

VAT on a quote

  • Not registered? Don't add VAT, and say "No VAT: we're not VAT registered", so the customer doesn't wonder whether it'll be added later. You must register if your taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12 months (gov.uk).
  • Registered? Show the subtotal, VAT at 20% and the total, or say clearly "all prices include VAT". Homeowners compare the bottom line, so show it with VAT.
  • Energy-saving materials: installing things like heat pumps, solar panels and insulation in homes is zero-rated until 31 March 2027, then 5% (HMRC notice 708/6). Check the notice for what qualifies.

A worked example

A plumber has looked at two cold bedroom radiators for a homeowner and agreed the job at the house. This is what the quote maker writes from the example placeholders (with the redecoration and powerflush exclusions ticked):

Hale Plumbing & Heating · 07700 900123

QUOTE  No. Q-0042    Date: 27 October 2026    Valid until: 26 November 2026
For: Sam Patel    Job address: 12 Elm Road, Leeds

1. The job
   Replace two bedroom radiators

2. What's included
   1. Drain down and refill the heating system, add inhibitor
   2. Supply and fit two 600 x 1000 mm double panel radiators in both bedrooms
   3. Fit new thermostatic radiator valves to both
   4. Remove old radiators and take them away

3. Not included
   - Redecoration after the work (we make good to a ready-to-paint finish only)
   - System flush or powerflush

4. This price assumes
   - Existing pipework is 15 mm copper in good condition
   - Parking on the drive

5. Price
   Labour, 1 day                     £320.00
   Radiators, valves and inhibitor   £286.40
   TOTAL                             £606.40
   No VAT: we're not VAT registered.

6. Payment
   Full amount: due within 7 days of completion. Bank transfer to [sort code, account number, reference].

7. Changes and extra work
   Any change to the work above will be agreed in writing, with a price, before we do it.

8. Your right to cancel
   You can cancel this contract within 14 days of accepting it, without giving a reason. See the attached cancellation information and form.
   If you'd like us to start within the 14 days, please tell us in writing. If you then cancel, you'll pay for the work done up to that point.

9. To go ahead
   Reply to this quote (Q-0042) saying "I accept", or sign below. This quote is valid until 26 November 2026.

   Signed: ____________________    Date: __________

And the message to send with it:

Hi Sam, thanks for showing me the job. Here's my quote (Q-0042) for the job we discussed: £606.40. It lists what's included and what isn't, so there are no surprises. It's valid until 26 November 2026. Any questions, just ask. To go ahead, reply "I accept".

Using ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude to write quotes

AI is good at turning rough site notes or a voice note into a tidy quote. It's bad at prices: it will happily invent a labour rate or a material cost. So give it your numbers and tell it not to make any up. A prompt that works:

Write a quote for a homeowner from my notes below. UK, I'm not VAT registered.
Use these sections: the job, what's included (numbered, specific), not included, this price assumes, price (itemised, my figures only), payment, changes and extra work, how to accept. Valid 30 days.
Do NOT invent prices, sizes or specs: if something's missing, write [?].
Then list anything vague a customer could argue about.

Notes: [paste your notes or voice-note transcript]
Prices: [labour, materials, other]

Leave out the customer's phone number and anything they wouldn't want shared; you can add those after.

FAQ

Is a quote legally binding in the UK?

Once the customer accepts it, it's a contract, and a "quote" is generally treated as a fixed price for the work described. An estimate is a best guess that can change, which is why it should say so and give an upper limit.

How long should a quote be valid for?

30 days is common. Use a shorter period if material prices are moving, and say what happens after: "after this date we'll re-check prices before confirming".

Can I charge for a quote?

Yes, if you tell the customer before the visit. Some trades charge for detailed quotes or surveys and refund the fee if the customer goes ahead. Say so when they book.

What if I find something unexpected once I start?

That's what the "hidden problems" exclusion and the changes clause are for. Stop, show the customer, give them a price in writing, and only carry on once they agree. Our Pro change-order skill writes that message.

Do I have to give homeowners a cancellation form?

If you agree the contract away from your business premises (usually at their home) and it's over £42, yes: give them the cancellation information and a cancellation form. If you don't, they can cancel for up to 12 months longer. Urgent repairs they called you out for are an exception.

Should I show VAT separately?

If you're VAT registered, show the subtotal, VAT and total, or say clearly that prices include VAT. If you're not registered, say so on the quote.
