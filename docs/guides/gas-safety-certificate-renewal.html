Gas safety certificate renewal: the next due date, worked out

For landlords keeping on top of the yearly check, and for gas engineers who want their landlord customers to rebook on time. A calculator that gives the due date, the early window that keeps it and the tenant-copy deadline, then writes the reminder or access letter for you.

Updated 29 October 2026 · England, Scotland and Wales · Checked against the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 on legislation.gov.uk and HSE's landlord guidance · General information, not legal advice

The short answer: a landlord's gas appliances and flues must be checked by a Gas Safe registered engineer at least every 12 months. The next check is due 12 months after the last one. You can have it done up to 2 months early and keep the same due date (the 2018 "MOT-style" rule). Give existing tenants a copy of the record within 28 days of the check, and new tenants before they move in.

How the dates are read: the due date is the same date 12 months after the check (a check on 29 February is due on 28 February). The early window is the 2 months ending with the due date, so for a 15 March due date it opens on 16 January. HSE's leaflet describes it as "10 to 12 calendar months after the previous check". If your engineer prints a different expiry date, go by the earlier of the two.

Boiler and fire on different dates?

If a boiler was checked in March and a gas fire was added in October, you'd have two due dates in the same home and two visits a year. Regulation 36A gives you two ways to line them up:

  • Bring one forward: have the later appliance checked early, with the earlier one. You lose the months it was early, once, and from then on both share a date.
  • Push one back, once: for each appliance, one time only, you can check it up to 2 months after its due date, to align it with another appliance in the same property. Note this on the record or in your file, so it's clear it was alignment and not a missed check.

Most engineers check every appliance at the same visit, which keeps everything on one date from the start.

PaperworkThe record, kept by the landlord, copied to tenantsA service record or benchbook entry, for the warranty

HSE's own words: "You should not assume that an annual service inspection meets the safety check requirement, or that a safety check will, on its own, be sufficient to provide effective maintenance." Landlords still have a separate duty to keep everything maintained in a safe condition.

When the tenant won't let the engineer in

You must not force entry. What the law asks is that you take all reasonable steps, and HSE says its inspectors look for at least three attempts. Its recommended steps:

  1. After a missed visit, leave a note saying the engineer called, why, and how to rearrange.
  2. Write explaining that the check is a legal requirement and is for the tenant's own safety.
  3. Offer the tenant the chance to choose their own appointment time.
  4. Keep everything: copies of letters, texts, call notes, the engineer's "no access" card. HSE strongly advises a record of all correspondence.

The calculator writes all three letters (pick them in the "Message" list). Start early in the window, so there's time for three attempts before the due date. If it's still impossible, get advice on what the tenancy allows; it's ultimately for a court to decide whether you did enough.

For gas engineers: reminders that fill your diary

Landlord checks repeat every year on a known date, and the 2-month window means customers can book early without losing anything. That makes them easy to plan around the busy heating season. A simple routine:

  1. Before you leave the job, tell the landlord the next due date and that you'll remind them.
  2. About 10 weeks before the due date, send the email reminder (in the calculator). It says they can book from the window date without losing months.
  3. When the window opens, send the text version if they haven't booked.
  4. 4 weeks before, a final nudge. The calculator's .ics file puts all three dates in your calendar.
  5. On the day, send the record straight away, so the landlord can give the tenant their copy within 28 days.

For homeowners, the same routine works for the annual boiler service: the calculator has an email and a text that fits in one SMS. Only send reminders to customers who've agreed to hear from you, and include a way to opt out (for example "reply STOP and I won't remind you again").

What if the check is late?

Book it straight away. The new 12 months run from the day it's done. Missing it is a breach of the regulations, which HSE enforces: its landlord guide says you risk prosecution and "imprisonment or a fine ... or both, for each offence", with an unlimited fine in the Crown Court. An overdue check doesn't mean an appliance is unsafe, but if an engineer finds an appliance dangerous and disconnects it, it must not be used again until it's been fixed.

Gas is one of several safety duties for landlords. Electrical safety checks, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and (in England) the new Renters' Rights rules have their own requirements: see gov.uk for each.

FAQ

How long is a landlord gas safety certificate valid for?

The law doesn't give the record an expiry date as such: it requires each appliance and flue to be checked at intervals of not more than 12 months. So the next check is due 12 months after the last one, which is the date most engineers print as the renewal date.

Can I renew a gas safety certificate early without losing months?

Yes, in the 2 months ending with the due date (regulation 36A, since 6 April 2018). Due 15 March? A check any time from 16 January keeps the next due date at 15 March the following year. Earlier than that, the 12 months restart from the visit.

When do tenants have to get a copy?

Existing tenants within 28 days of the check, new tenants before they move in. For lets of 28 days or less you can display it prominently instead. A digital copy is common; keep proof you sent it.

What happens if a landlord's gas safety certificate expires?

It's a breach of the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998, which HSE can prosecute (fines, imprisonment or both, per offence). Book a check straight away: the next due date then runs from the day it's done.

Is a boiler service the same as a gas safety check?

No. The check produces the legal record; a service is maintenance to the manufacturer's instructions. Many engineers do both in one visit, but one doesn't replace the other.

What if the tenant won't let the engineer in?

Never force entry. Leave a note after a missed visit, write to explain it's a legal safety check, offer them the chance to pick a time, and keep a record of every attempt. HSE looks for at least three.

Does a tenant's own gas cooker need checking?

Not the appliance itself if it's theirs, but the landlord is still responsible for the pipework. HSE suggests reminding tenants to have their own appliances checked, and offering to include them at the same visit.

Does this apply to holiday lets, lodgers and HMOs?

Yes to holiday lets, HMOs, bedsits and B&Bs: HSE lists them all. A lodger in your own home counts if you let to them under a licence. Leases of 7 years or more aren't covered by the landlord duty.
