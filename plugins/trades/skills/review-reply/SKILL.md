# Replying to reviews

For a local trades business, the reviews page is the shop window. Future
customers read the replies more closely than the reviews: a calm, specific
reply to a one-star review can win more work than another five-star one.
An angry reply loses work for years.

The owner is usually upset when they ask for help. Your job is to write
the reply they'll be glad they sent next month.

## Step 1: Read the review properly

Ask the owner to paste the review and tell you (briefly):

1. **What actually happened**, from their side, with dates.
2. **Is this a real customer?** Can they match it to a job?
3. **What they're willing to offer**: a return visit, a partial refund,
   a call, nothing.
4. **Platform**: some have a character limit or let you reply only once.

Then sort the review:

| Type | Example | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | "Brilliant job, tidy and on time" | Thank them, specific detail, short |
| Mixed | "Good work but turned up late twice" | Thank, own the weak point, what's changed |
| Negative, fair | "Leak came back after a week" | Own it, fix offer, take it offline |
| Negative, unfair or inaccurate | "Overcharged!" (price was agreed in writing) | Calm facts, no argument, offline |
| Not a customer / fake | No matching job, competitor-style wording | Short neutral reply + report it |
| Abusive / discriminatory / private info | Threats, slurs, addresses | Report it, a minimal reply or none |

## Step 2: Rules for every public reply

- **Write for the next customer**, not the reviewer. Would a stranger
  reading this want to hire the business?
- **Short**: 40–120 words. Long replies look defensive.
- **Use the reviewer's first name** if it's shown. Sign off with the
  owner's first name and role ("Dave, owner"). A real person beats "The
  Management".
- **No private details**: never post the customer's address, job details
  they wouldn't want public, prices they paid (unless they posted them),
  or anything about their home, health or family.
- **No arguing line by line.** State one or two key facts at most, neutrally.
- **Never call the reviewer a liar**, and don't hint that they're mad,
  drunk or a competitor. If it's fake, say you can't find a record of the
  job, and report it.
- **Never offer money or discounts in exchange for changing or removing a
  review.** Platforms ban it. In the UK, the Digital Markets, Competition
  and Consumers Act 2024 bans fake reviews and concealed incentivised
  reviews. In the US, the FTC's rule on fake reviews (in force from
  October 2024) bans buying reviews and review suppression. You can fix the
  problem and let the customer decide whether to update their review.
- **Move it offline**: give a direct phone number or email for the owner
  (not a generic inbox).
- **Don't admit legal liability** for damage or injury. If the review
  alleges damage, injury, a gas or electrical safety issue, or threatens
  legal action, keep the reply minimal ("We take this seriously and have
  contacted you directly") and tell the owner to speak to their insurer
  before saying more. If there's a live safety risk (a gas smell, exposed
  wiring), the owner should contact the customer today, not just reply
  online.

## Step 3: Templates by type

Adapt these. Don't paste them unchanged, or every reply sounds the same.

**Positive**: thank them + one specific detail from the job + a warm
close. "Thanks, Sarah. That kitchen rewire had some tricky old cabling, so
we're really pleased it all went smoothly. Enjoy the new lights! Dave"

**Mixed**: thank them + acknowledge the issue plainly + what's changed.
"Thanks for the kind words about the finish, Tom, and you're right that we
were late twice. That's not good enough. We now text an arrival time on the
morning of every job. Dave"

**Negative, fair**: apologise for the specific problem (not "any
inconvenience") + what you'll do + offline contact. "Sorry, Priya, a leak
coming back a week later isn't the standard we work to. I've called and left
a message, and we'll come back free of charge to put it right. You can reach
me directly on [number]. Dave"

**Negative, unfair**: thank them for the feedback + one neutral fact +
offline invitation. "Thanks for your feedback, Mark. We agreed the price in
writing before starting and didn't add any extras, but I'm sorry you're
unhappy with the cost. I'd be glad to go through the invoice with you by
phone: [number]. Dave"

**Can't find the customer**: "Hi [name], we can't find a job under this
name or for the work described, so we'd like to understand what happened.
Could you contact me on [number] so we can look into it? Dave". Then help
the owner report it: Google Business Profile lets owners report reviews
that break its policies (fake engagement, off-topic, conflict of interest,
harassment). Other platforms have similar flags. Removal isn't guaranteed.

## Step 4: Private follow-up

Draft a short private message (SMS/email) to the customer for negative
reviews: acknowledge, offer the fix or a call, and give a time. Don't
mention the review rating and don't ask them to change it.

## Step 5: Ask happy customers for reviews

Most happy customers never review unless asked. Draft a short message to
send on the day the job finishes:

"Hi [first name], thanks again for having us to [job]. If you're happy
with the work, a quick review really helps a small business like ours:
[link]. If anything isn't right, please tell me first so I can sort it.
[Owner name]"

Rules: ask every customer, not just the ones you expect to be positive
(selective asking can be treated as misleading under fake-review rules),
offer no incentive for a review, and never write reviews for customers.

## Quality checklist

- [ ] The reply would make a stranger more likely to hire the business.
- [ ] 40–120 words, signed by a named person.
- [ ] No private customer details, no accusations, no sarcasm.
- [ ] No incentive offered for changing or removing the review.
- [ ] No admission of legal liability where damage or injury is alleged.
- [ ] An offline contact route is included for negative reviews.
- [ ] Facts match what the owner told you; nothing is invented.

## Output

1. **Public reply**, ready to paste (and a shorter version if the platform
   limits length).
2. **Private follow-up message** (negative or mixed reviews).
3. **Should you report it?** Yes/no and why, with the platform's reporting route.
4. **One lesson** for the business, if the review points to a fixable
   pattern (arrival times, communication, tidying up).
